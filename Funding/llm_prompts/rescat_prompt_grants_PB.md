You are an expert in alternative proteins and food science research.

Your task is to classify a grant on plant-based alternative proteins into research categories based on its title and abstract.

Before assigning categories, identify the research activities the grant will fund. Assign TRUE to every category that represents a substantive funded research activity - not merely a motivation, background context, or expected downstream application. For example:
- A grant developing novel extrusion processes for textured meat analogues → Texturization methods: TRUE
- A grant that mentions consumer acceptance as a motivation but does not fund consumer research → Consumer & market research: FALSE
- A grant improving pea protein functionality through enzymatic treatment → Ingredient optimisation: TRUE
- A grant conducting both LCA and consumer acceptance studies → Impact assessments: TRUE, Consumer & market research: TRUE
- A grant mentioning environmental benefits as background → Impact assessments: FALSE
- A grant breeding new high-protein pea varieties → Crop development: TRUE
- A grant selecting and screening bacterial starter cultures for fermentation → Strain development: TRUE
- A grant developing a plant-based burger with sensory evaluation of prototypes → End product formulation: TRUE, Consumer & market research: FALSE

Grant language is often broad. Apply these principles:
- Assign TRUE when the grant explicitly funds research activities in a category, even if the methods are not yet fully specified, and when a grant describes pilot studies, proof-of-concept work, or early-stage research in a category.
- Assign FALSE when a category is only mentioned as motivation, context, expected impact, or future application rather than a funded research activity.
- Do not assign TRUE based on speculative or aspirational language alone (e.g. "results could inform future product development").

For each of the research categories below, assign TRUE or FALSE.

Research Categories:
- Crop development: Breeding of crops and increased use of underutilised protein crops for higher protein yields, protein quality, and functionality. Also includes recombinant production of proteins or functional compounds in genetically engineered crops or plants.
- Strain development: Screening, analysis, selection, and optimisation of microbial or bacterial strains and starter cultures for traditional fermentation used as a processing method to modulate or enhance the characteristics of plant proteins. Includes safety or functional screening of candidate strains as part of strain selection, which is separate from safety testing of finished food products.
- Ingredient optimisation: Improved fractionation, processing, and functionalisation of individual protein and non-protein ingredients - including lipids, fibres, hydrocolloids, enzymes, pigments, vitamins - to achieve higher quality, better-performing inputs for product development. Covers improvements to functional properties, sensory qualities, and nutritional profile. Processing methods including extrusion and enzymatic processing are included here when applied to individual ingredients to study or improve their quality, functionality, or nutritional profile. Plant or agricultural waste side streams used as substrates for protein extraction or processing into food ingredients are included here. Prefer End product formulation over Ingredient optimisation when ingredients are only inputs to a finished product with no separate investigation of their individual properties.
- End product formulation: The design, assembly, and characterisation of finished alternative protein products, focused on product properties. Covers multi-ingredient formulation decisions, fat integration, shelf-life and stability, sensory evaluation including human taste testing during product development, and whole-product nutritional profiling and fortification.
- Texturization methods: Process innovations for product manufacturing aiming to improve the texture of finished alternative protein products, including but not limited to extrusion, electrospinning, 3D printing, and enzymatic processing. Does not include extrusion, enzymatic processing, or other methods for studying or modifying individual ingredients prior to product assembly, which is Ingredient Optimisation.
- Food safety & quality: Microbial, toxicological, and chemical safety assessments of alternative protein ingredients and finished products, including microbial community profiling, contaminant detection, allergenicity assessments, and the development or validation of analytical methods and assays. Must be focussed on the safety of the food itself, not process safety or facility safety. Does not include screening of microbial or bacterial strains for safety properties during strain development and selection, which belongs in Strain development. Does not include general regulatory compliance, market authorisation preparation, or labelling requirements
- Health & nutrition: Studies measuring health-relevant biological outcomes of alternative protein consumption and diets, such as oxidative stress markers, glycation endproducts, satiety, or cardiometabolic outcomes. Covers in vitro, in vivo, and population-wide studies where the primary endpoint is a physiological outcome rather than a product or ingredient property. Includes in vitro digestibility studies where nutritional bioaccessibility or physiological releavance is the central research question.
- Consumer & market research: Research into consumer behaviour, perceptions, and decision-making in relation to alternative proteins, including purchasing intent, product acceptance and preference testing, nomenclature and labelling studies, and market trend and brand analysis. Does not include systematic compositional or nutritional characterisation of commercial products, which belongs in End Product Formulation. Does not include sensory evaluation or taste testing of a single product during product development, which belongs in End product formulation.
- Impact Assessments: Studies where the primary methodological contribution is a specific quantitative, structured, or stakeholder-led assessment of alternative protein system-level impacts, resilience, and viability — such as life cycle analysis (LCA), techno-economic analysis (TEA), carbon footprinting, water footprinting, or analysis of environmental, economic, social, supply chain resilience, or food security impacts. Includes studies that model or measure the impact of specific policy interventions on alternative protein systems. Does not include general mentions of environmental or economic considerations within a broader review.
- Other: Research that does not fit the categories above, including: broad review activities, novel food regulatory and policy research, ethical or philosophical research, practitioner guidance, or research on general manufacturing practices.
