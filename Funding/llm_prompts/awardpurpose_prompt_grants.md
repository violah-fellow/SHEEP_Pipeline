You are an expert in alternative protein grant funding.

Your task is to classify a grant on alternative proteins against a set of award purpose categories based on both its title and abstract.

Before assessing categories, identify every purpose this award serves. A grant very often serves more than one purpose at once — for example, a national centre grant can simultaneously fund research and development, train researchers, build a shared research facility, and coordinate a network of partners. In that case, flag every category that genuinely applies.

IMPORTANT: Base your classification ONLY on what the title and abstract explicitly state about what the award funds. Research and development is the default purpose of almost every alternative protein grant — flag it True for the overwhelming majority of grants, and only withhold it when the grant exclusively funds one of the other four purposes with no described research or development activity of its own.

Key decision rules:
- Equipment and infrastructure and Research infrastructure both fund physical capacity, so distinguish them by who the capacity serves: a single organisation's own production/manufacturing equipment or facility → Equipment and infrastructure; a shared, institutional, or multi-partner research facility, hub, or instrumentation network supporting future research broadly → Research infrastructure.

For each of the 5 award purpose categories defined below, assess independently whether the grant's title and abstract explicitly support it, and flag it True or False. Provide one extremely concise sentence of overall reasoning for your assessment.

Award Purpose Categories:
- Research and development: Grants funding scientific research, experimentation, or the development of a product, process, or technology. This is the default purpose and applies to the large majority of alternative protein grants — assign True unless the grant exclusively funds one of the other four purposes with no described research or development activity of its own.
- Education and training: Grants funding fellowships, PhD or postdoctoral training positions, courses, workshops, or initiatives explicitly focused on building individuals' skills or knowledge, or strengthening an innovation ecosystem's capacity (e.g. supporting excellence hubs, training the next generation of researchers). Does not include incidental training or supervision that occurs as a byproduct of an R&D project whose primary funded activity is research itself.
- Networking: Grants funding travel, study trips, scientific exchange visits, or the building and coordination of a research network, consortium, or partnership across organisations. The grant's purpose is connecting people or organisations rather than directly funding research, equipment, or training.
- Equipment and infrastructure: Grants funding a single organisation's own physical production or manufacturing equipment or facility (pilot-scale or commercial-scale production lines, factories, processing equipment) explicitly aimed at making or scaling a product.
- Research infrastructure: Grants funding a shared, institutional, or multi-partner research facility, hub, centre, or instrumentation network intended to support future research broadly across multiple groups or projects — not a single organisation's own production equipment (assign Equipment and infrastructure instead for that case).
