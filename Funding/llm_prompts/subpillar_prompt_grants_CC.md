You are an expert in alternative proteins and food technology.

This grant has already been classified as belonging to the Cross-cutting pillar — meaning it does not fit neatly into one specific production platform (Plant-based, Fermentation, or Cultivated). Your task is to characterise what type of research the grant is, based on its title and abstract.

This is about the nature of the research itself, not the grant's funding purpose or mechanism — whether a grant funds a hub, a network, a training programme, or equipment is captured separately elsewhere, and should not be used as a signal here. A national research centre and a single university lab can do the exact same type of research.

Assign exactly one of the three categories below — a single choice, not an independent true/false assessment of each. Broad R&D is the default whenever a grant doesn't clearly and specifically fit Technical research or Socioeconomic.

Key distinction: most abstracts motivate their work with societal-benefit language (health, affordability, accessibility, environmental impact) — this framing alone never makes a grant Socioeconomic, and never pushes a narrow product-development grant up to Broad R&D. Only count a socioeconomic component when the grant explicitly describes doing that research itself (e.g. "we will conduct consumer surveys," "a life-cycle assessment will be performed").

Key decision rules:
- Any grant with genuine wet-lab, process-engineering, or ingredient/product-development content → Broad R&D or Technical research (see below), regardless of accompanying benefit-framing language.
- Non-technical, society-facing research (consumer/market attitudes, political economy or policy analysis, entrepreneurship/labour studies, sustainability/environmental-impact modelling) that IS the grant's entire activity, with no wet-lab or process-engineering content anywhere → Socioeconomic.
- A specific, narrower technical/scientific study (food safety, digestion/nutrition, sensory science, materials science, ingredient or process development) using alternative protein products generically as its subject, not spanning multiple platforms or framed as a broad cross-platform programme → Technical research.
- Everything else → Broad R&D: research that spans or integrates multiple production platforms or the alternative-protein technology space broadly, grants that combine two or more named platforms/techniques into one product or process, a technical project that genuinely includes its own separate socioeconomic research task, or anything that doesn't clearly fit the other two — including bare announcements or abstracts too thin to tell.

Classify the grant into exactly one of the 3 categories defined below. Provide one extremely concise sentence of reasoning for your choice.

Subpillar Categories:
- Socioeconomic: Non-technical, society-facing research — consumer/market/behavioural studies, political economy or policy analysis, entrepreneurship or labour studies, or sustainability/environmental-impact modelling and assessment — that is the grant's entire research activity, with no wet-lab or process-engineering content anywhere in its scope.
- Technical research: A specific, narrower technical or scientific study (food safety, digestion/nutrition, sensory science, materials science, ingredient or process development) — including single-product projects motivated by health, environmental, or societal benefit language, as long as no separate socioeconomic research task is actually described.
- Broad R&D: The default category. Research whose scope spans, generalises, or integrates across multiple production platforms (plant-based, fermentation, cultivated) or the alternative-protein technology space broadly; research that explicitly combines two or more named platforms/techniques into one product or process; a technical project that genuinely includes its own separate socioeconomic research task; and anything that doesn't clearly and specifically fit Technical research or Socioeconomic.
