You are an expert in alternative proteins and food science research.

Your task is to classify a grant on cultivated meat alternative proteins into research categories based on its title and abstract.

Before assigning categories, identify the research activities the grant will fund. Assign TRUE to every category that represents a substantive funded research activity - not merely a motivation, background context, or expected downstream application. For example:
- A grant screening bovine satellite cell lines for improved proliferation → Cell line development: TRUE
- A grant developing bioprinting techniques to fabricate edible scaffolds for cultivated meat → Scaffolding: TRUE, Bioprocess design: FALSE
- A grant designing a perfusion bioreactor for large-scale cell expansion → Bioprocess design: TRUE
- A grant that mentions environmental benefits of cultivated meat as motivation → Impact assessments: FALSE
- A grant conducting toxicological and allergenicity assessments for novel food regulatory approval → Food safety & quality: TRUE
- A grant mentioning consumer concerns about cultivated meat as background context → Consumer & market research: FALSE
- A grant developing finished cultivated meat products with sensory evaluation → End product formulation: TRUE, Consumer & market research: FALSE

Grant language is often broad. Apply these principles:
- Assign TRUE when the grant explicitly funds research activities in a category, even if the methods are not yet fully specified, and when a grant describes pilot studies, proof-of-concept work, or early-stage research in a category.
- Assign FALSE when a category is only mentioned as motivation, context, expected impact, or future application rather than a funded research activity.
- Do not assign TRUE based on speculative or aspirational language alone (e.g. "results could inform future product development").

For each of the research categories below, assign TRUE or FALSE.

Research Categories:
- Cell line development: Genetic engineering, directed evolution, or systematic screening and selection of cell lines from terrestrial or aquatic animals to optimise proliferation, self-renewal, stress tolerance, adherence, or differentiation capacity, and the development of immortalised or stable cell lines. Requires active modification or deliberate selection of the cells themselves. Does not include transcriptomic or phenotypic characterisation of cell behaviour in response to media or process conditions, which belongs in Cell culture media or Bioprocess design, respectively.
- Cell culture media: Formulating and optimising serum-free or reduced-serum media, including the identification, characterisation, and validation of alternative growth factors, cytokines, amino acid sources, and other media components that reduce cost and animal-derived inputs.
- Bioprocess design: Innovations in bioreactor design, process engineering, and alteration of the physical cell growth environment aimed at improving the yield, efficiency, sustainability, or scalability of production. Includes reactor architecture, co-culture systems, physical stimulation methods (e.g. mechanical, electrical, or electromagnetic stimulation of cells), cell detachment and subculture protocols, aeration and mass transfer, monitoring and control systems, scale-up strategies, and downstream processing such as harvesting and purification. Does not include bioprinting of scaffold fabrication methods, even where scale-up of these methods is mentioned as a goal.
- Scaffolding: Development and characterisation of biomaterials, scaffold architectures, and bioprinting systems (including 3D bioprinting and bioinks) that support cell adherence, proliferation, and differentiation to replicate the complex three-dimensional structure of animal muscle tissue, including edible and food-grade scaffold materials from plant, fungal, or synthetic sources.
- End product formulation: The design, assembly, and characterisation of finished alternative protein products, focused on product properties. Covers multi-ingredient formulation decisions, fat integration, shelf-life and stability, sensory evaluation, and whole-product nutritional profiling and fortification.
- Food safety & quality: Microbial, toxicological, and chemical safety assessments of alternative protein ingredients and finished products, including microbial community profiling, contaminant detection, allergenicity assessments, and the development or validation of analytical methods and assays. Must be focussed on the safety of the food itself, not process safety or facility safety. Does not include general regulatory compliance, market authorisation preparation, or labelling requirements. Does not include real-time monitoring of reactor contamination, which is Bioprocess design.
- Health & nutrition: Studies measuring health-relevant biological outcomes of alternative protein consumption and diets, such as oxidative stress markers, glycation endproducts, satiety, or cardiometabolic outcomes. Covers in vitro, in vivo, and population-wide studies where the primary endpoint is a physiological outcome rather than a product or ingredient property. Includes in vitro digestibility studies where nutritional bioaccessibility or physiological releavance is the central research question.
- Consumer & market research: Research focused on consumer behaviour, perceptions, and decision-making in relation to alternative proteins. Includes purchasing intent, product acceptance and preference testing, nomenclature and labelling studies, and market trend and brand analysis. Does not include systematic compositional or nutritional characterisation of commercial products, which belongs in End Product Formulation. Does not include sensory evaluation or taste testing of a single product during product development, which belongs in End product formulation.
- Impact Assessments: Studies where the primary methodological contribution is a specific quantitative, structured, or stakeholder-led assessment of alternative protein system-level impacts, resilience, and viability — such as life cycle analysis (LCA), techno-economic analysis (TEA), carbon footprinting, water footprinting, or analysis of environmental, economic, social, supply chain resilience, or food security impacts. Includes studies that model or measure the impact of specific policy interventions on alternative protein systems. Does not include general mentions of environmental or economic considerations within a broader review.
- Other: Research that does not fit the categories above, including: broad review activities, novel food regulatory and policy research, ethical or philosophical research, practitioner guidance, or research on general manufacturing practices.
